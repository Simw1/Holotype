# Holotype grime register: fact-check of 12 specimens

Checked 16 Nov 2026 against the cited URLs (fetched live). Discogs and MusicBrainz were not used. Direct fetches of en.wikipedia.org worked, so no mirrors were needed apart from the IPFS URLs already in the data.

Key: **OK** = the cited page supports it. **ERROR** = the page contradicts it or does not say it. **UNVERIFIED** = could not be confirmed from the cited page.

---

## 1. musical-mob-pulse-x (Musical Mob (Youngstar), *Pulse X*, Inspired Sounds, Mar 2002)

**Verified**
- Date, label and format: Wikipedia says it was "first released on 12" vinyl in March 2002", with a wider release in July 2002, on Inspired Sounds. FACT's credit line is "MUSICAL MOB 'PULSE X' (INSPIRED SOUNDS, 2002)". The Wikipedia infobox credits "Youngstar", and its text says "producer Youngstar of the crew Musical Mob". No catalogue number is given anywhere, so `cat: null` is correct.
- sound[0], the 16-bar loop structure (2-step for 8 bars, then kick and snare for 8; no intro; fade-out; voice sample every 32 bars): OK (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulse_X).
- sound[1]: OK. SPIN has "petulant, overdriven bass bleeps and nervous handclaps", "lo-fi … minimalism" and "gaping empty spaces".
- sound[2]: OK as a paraphrase. FACT has "a deconstruction of breakbeat garage down to its blackened foundations" and "twisted sense of minimalism".
- sound[3]: OK. Ransom Note quotes Youngstar: "I would make the drum pattern on rebirth then sample a bassline … a sequencer called digital orchestrator".
- context[0]: OK. FACT, Tom Lea, 22 Apr 2012 (first posted 2011), no. 01.
- context[1]: OK. Wikipedia has "widely considered to be the first ever grime beat", "year zero", and Bok Bok's 2014 phrase "simplistic, brutalist genius". The minimalism "set the foundations" for a more MC-centric genre; the data's "credited with making" is slightly stronger than the source.
- context[2]: OK. Ransom Note: "the first 8 bar track released which sold out within the first week in Blackmarket"; "sell a crazy amount of units back when vinyl used to sell".
- context[3]: OK. Byline is Philip Sherburne, 4 Dec 2012. SPIN says "a case to be made that 'Pulse X' is grime's founding track" and "Wiley's 'Eskimo,' another contender, didn't get released until later in 2002".
- type.basis: FACT no. 1, SPIN and Wikipedia all OK.

**Issues / cautions**
- type.basis: "Earliest-dated record in this set that sources widely call the first grime beat" is true only because of the qualifier. Two other specimens are dated earlier or the same month: Plasticman *Venom* ("early 2002", which its maker calls "8-bar grime") and *Oi!* (promo 2001; commercial 4 Mar 2002). This bears on "first 8-bar track released" and on priority, and the panel should be told. It is not a factual error.

**Unverified**: nothing material.

## 2. wiley-eskimo (Wiley, *Eskimo*, Wiley Kat Recordings, Jul 2002)

**Verified**
- Date, label and format: Wikipedia (Eskimo_(grime_beat)) gives "Released: July 2002", label Wiley Kat Recordings, 12" vinyl. FACT 2010's credit line is "(WILEY KAT RECORDS 12", 2002)". No catalogue number is known, so `null` is correct.
- sound[0]: OK. Complex, Son Raw, 6 Aug 2019: "rough breakbeat", "plinky pentatonic melodies, clipped flutes, and hollow digital basslines".
- sound[1]: OK. Mixmag, Dan Hancox, 6 Jun 2018: "minimal drum skirmishes, some artificial synth stabs", "hollow metal pole rolling around a construction yard". "Heavy bass / off-kilter rhythms" is Hancox describing eskibeat in general, not this track.
- sound[2]: OK. FACT 2010: "A gong, some Sino taps, and the sound of a hollow metal pole…"
- sound[3]: OK. MusicTech: "hollow bass dumps", "artificial synth stabs, with clipped flutes and loaded digital kick drums".
- context[1]: OK. Mixmag has "the first of his eski-oeuvre, and the most enduring", "no label, no manager, no publicist" and "over 10,000 copies on vinyl". Complex says it "allegedly sold 10,000 copies … out of local specialty shops like Rhythm Division".
- context[2]: OK. FACT 2012: "'Eskimo' is the definitive grime riddim", and it created the eski micro-genre.
- context[3]: OK. RBMA Daily, Emma Warren, 26 May 2015.
- type.basis, "SPIN names it the rival contender": OK. SPIN calls it "another contender".

**Errors**
- context[0] (https://www.fabriclondon.com/posts/hyperdub-archive-eski-beat-an-interview-with-wiley-part-2): Wiley does say he made it "Christmas '99 or 2000" and that "'Eskimo' did 10,000". The **car boot** remark, however, is about his releases in general ("I've sold well over 50,000 out of my car boot"), not about Eskimo. The specific car-boot link comes from Mixmag ("boot of a Vauxhall Corsa").
- type.basis, "Wiley named the eski sound after this run of records": this is **overstated**. In the Oct 2003 fabric/Hyperdub interview, part 1, Wiley says "I want call my sound 'eski beat'" and "I will have been the namer". In part 2 he says he "had the idea for all the cold names for the tunes". No cited source says outright that he named the sound *after* Eskimo or the cold-titled run. RBMA says it doesn't explain the name. Wikipedia calls Eskimo "an archetype" of eskibeat.

## 4. dizzee-rascal-i-luv-u (Dizzee Rascal, *I Luv U*, XL, 2002 test pressing; single 26 May 2003)

**Verified**
- Single date 26 May 2003, CD formats, producer Dizzee Rascal: OK. Wikipedia gives the label as "XL; Dirtee Stank".
- The 2002 test pressing played on pirate radio: OK (Wikipedia).
- sound[0]: OK. "a stuttering voice repeating the song's title", "bass-heavy rhythm".
- sound[1], sound[2]: OK (RBMA track-by-track: dirty bassline, the kick reused on "Wot U On?", the "What's Your Fantasy?" strings, emulating "Is That Your Chick?").
- sound[3] and context[3]: OK. Stereogum (14 Mar 2016) has "dark tale of teenage pregnancy and blackmail", "bass-heavy instrumental" and "one of the first grime singles to chart in the UK".
- context[0]: OK. UK no. 29, Indie no. 5, "first top forty hit".
- context[1]: OK. The Wire's "Anarchy in the U.K." analogy; *The Pitchfork 500* (2008).

**Errors**
- type.basis, "the first grime record in this set to reach the top 40": **wrong within the register's own data.** *Oi!* (more-fire-crew-oi-2002, a paralectotype of Grime in this set) reached no. 8 in March 2002 (OCC). *I Luv U* is Dizzee's first top 40, not the set's.
- context[2], "Self-produced at 16": the cited RBMA page **does not give his age**. It says he had "just dropped out of college". The page does support "made before the rest of the album" and the Deja Vu FM / NASTY Crew context.
- Label, minor: Wikipedia gives "XL; Dirtee Stank". Optional.

## 6. lethal-bizzle-pow-forward-2004 (Lethal Bizzle, *Pow! (Forward)*, Relentless, RELDX15, 25 Oct 2004 digital)

**Verified**
- Date: OK. Digital release 25 Oct 2004 on iTunes; physical 20 Dec 2004.
- Catalogue number RELDX15: OK (OCC and Wikipedia). Wikipedia's vinyl numbers are RELT 15 / RELTX 15 / RELTDJ 15.
- Producer Dexplicit: OK.
- context[0]: OK. OCC: Singles no. 11, first chart date 01/01/2005, 7 weeks; Dance no. 1.
- sound[0]: OK. "Eight-bar rally", no breaks or chorus, two alternating eight-bar loops.
- sound[2]: OK. Made in FL Studio in under an hour; Wikipedia says he "claims" to be among the first to use US hip-hop samples and 808s.
- context[1]: OK. MOBO Best Single 2005; bans in London and the South East; "Do not play Lethal Bizzle" T-shirts.
- context[2]: OK. More Fire Crew/Fire Camp, East Connection, Roll Deep (Flowdan), N.A.S.T.Y (D Double E).
- format, "vinyl version with a longer Hotshot verse": OK (16 bars).
- sound[3] and context[3]: OK. Reynolds, Spin, Aug 2005: "a massive jolt of sonic adrenaline", "frantic" Forward riddim, "grime's biggest U.K. hit to date", Funkmaster Flex on Hot 97.

**Minor attribution issue**
- sound[1] and type.basis: in RA (Christian Adofo, 2 Nov 2024) only "8-bar instrumental … stark, bouncy synthline … claps … militaristic fanfare" is RA's own description. The no-intro bass and the 808 being new to grime are **D'Explicit's own words** quoted in the review ("we didn't hear it in grime before"). Attribute them to the producer.
- Label, minor: Wikipedia gives "Lethal Bizzle Records and Relentless Records". OCC gives just "RELENTLESS", so the current value is defensible.

## 7. kano-ps-and-qs-2004 (Kano, *P's and Q's*, 679 Recordings, Nov 2004)

**Verified**
- Date Nov 2004 and producer DaVinChe: OK (Wikipedia *Home Sweet Home*). RBMA calls it his "2004 debut single".
- Label 679: OK. RBMA says it was "released by stalwart indie label 679". Fiddy (8 Nov 2004) has "P's & Q's remix (679 Recordings)".
- sound[0]: OK. PopMatters (Pierre Hamilton, 24 Mar 2005): a heavily synthesised backbeat that "darts between the left and right channels"; "the album's most palpably kinetic track".
- sound[1]: OK. Stereogum has "effortlessly riding the frantic beat" and "showcases how grime should sound sonically". The word "model" is a paraphrase.
- sound[2]: OK. Fiddy: "As good as the original, just crunk it."
- context[0], context[1]: OK. RBMA: "underground anthem … rising star". Fiddy: "playlisted on Choice FM".
- context[2]: OK. "later certified Gold in May 2025", mainly from streaming and downloads.

It does not document the Nov 2004 release, so don't use it as evidence for the first release. It does confirm DaVinChe as producer of P's and Q's.

## 9. wonder-what-dump-valve-2004 (Wonder, *What*, Dump Valve, 2004)

**Verified**

**Minor**

## Overall verdict

The register is largely accurate. All label, date, catalogue-number and chart figures checked out. There are two substantive errors in type.basis claims: *I Luv U* as the set's first top 40 (*Oi!* got there first), and *Run the Road* as a "major-label" survey (679 was independent). The eski-naming basis is overstated. About ten context or sound statements need rewording or re-citing, either to restore a hedge, fix who is being quoted, or move a claim to the URL that actually supports it.
