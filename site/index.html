Holotype

Early grime and its garage roots, 2000 to 2006

Sixty-eight real releases from the years grime emerged, each held as a register entry: sourced release facts, evidence graded by when it was published, a ten-character musical profile, and a proposed place in a type series that anchors the names grime and eskibeat to dated records. Garage records are included as an outgroup, so the boundary can be seen rather than asserted. An audit then tests what streaming platforms call the same records.

What is solid. Release facts and evidence come from published sources linked on every entry, each graded A (published at the time), B (a maker recalling later) or C (later journalism, reference works and shop listings). What the audit shows. What is provisional. Musical codes are drafts: some are supported by published descriptions, the rest inferred, and all await confirmation by ear. Type statuses are proposals for review, not decisions. No audio has been accessioned, so nothing is measured yet.

05 · Method

How an entry is built

Evidence is sealed and only ever added to; classification sits in a separate layer that can be revised. The biology terms give the evidence structure. They describe recordings, never people.

Evidence grades

APublished at the time (2002 to 2006): chart records, contemporary reviews, interviews and blogs.
BA maker or participant recalling later.
CLater journalism, reference works and shop listings.

Grades in v0.1 were assigned by rule from each source's date and type, then spot-checked. They need a full manual pass.

Type terms used here

HolotypeThe record a name was attached to when it was coined. On the evidence so far, none of these names has one.
LectotypeAn anchor chosen later, when no record was named at the time. Most genre names, grime included, need one.
ParalectotypeThe other early records in the same series once a lectotype is chosen.
ParatypeOther records cited alongside a holotype.
VoucherEvidence for the name, with no naming role.
TransitionalA record sources split between UK garage and grime; part of the grime series until the priority question is settled.
OutgroupA UK garage record used as a reference point, as an outgroup roots a tree in phylogenetics: it shows which characters grime inherited and which are new.
CandidateA proposed anchor too weakly sourced to designate yet.
Context artefactAn album, compilation, mixtape or DVD: evidence about circulation, not coded as one profile.

The ten characters (coding scheme v0.1, grime-specific)

Where the analogy stops

  • Musical characters are not genes. The ten characters are a coding scheme, versioned like the taxonomy, and this version is built for grime.
  • Inheritance is chosen. A producer decides what to take, so links record credited reuse, versions and samples, not mutation.
  • Much of what makes grime grime is not in the audio: pirate radio, clashes, crews and place. That enters as graded evidence from people and documents.
